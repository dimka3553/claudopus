The tour

Watch Claudopus work.

One chat, three requests, none of them waiting in line. Then the part that matters after the work is done: where each result goes, and how you pick it up again.

Results, step by step

The work is done. Where did the answer go?

In Claude Code alone, a result is a message in a chat. Ten messages later it is far up the page, and next week it is in a chat you cannot find. Claudopus keeps each one in a list of its own.

1 · A task finishes

What it found is kept, by itself.

When a task ends, Claudopus saves what it wrote for you: the text, and a copy of each picture it names. A short “done” is not kept, so the list holds only what is worth opening.

You do nothing for this. The Results button in the panel's header counts them.

2 · The list

What waits for you first, then the newest.

At the top is Waiting on you: the results that end with something only you can decide. Under it are the five newest, then Earlier and Archived, folded away. Press a title to open that result.

One row is one piece of work. If you sent it back for another round, the row shows the newest round. Archive takes a row out of the way, and Bring back returns it. Nothing is deleted.

3 · Decide

Answer with one press.

Open a result that waits, and each question in it has Yes, No and Not now. Yes or No is sent to the chat with the name of the result, and the chat acts on it. Not now sends nothing and sets the question aside.

So after an hour away you do not read five chats to find what is stuck. You open one list, and what waits is at the top.

4 · Open one

The finding first, then the pictures, then all of it.

A result opens in a tab of its own. Finding is its first sentences, so you know in five seconds what came out. Pictures shows what the task made, one at a time. In full is the whole text.

Continue from this puts a line naming the result in the chat's box. You add what you want next and send it, today or next month.

5 · It stays yours

Plain files, on your machine.

Each result is a folder under ~/.claude/claudopus/reports: the text as it was written, and its pictures. It outlives the chat that made it. Read it with any editor, search it with grep, delete it when you like.

The chat is told the newest results with every message, so “what did the export task find?” gets an answer from the result, not from memory. If you name your other Macs in the settings, results are copied between them.

Why this is the part you end up using most.

You can walk away.

Start five tasks and go to lunch. What each one found is in the list when you come back, with what needs your decision on top.

Your chat stays small.

Long results live in Results, not in the chat. The chat keeps room for what you say next, and forgets less.

Work does not start from zero.

Last week's finding is one press away, and Continue from this hands it to a new task.

Two lines, and it is on in every chat.
