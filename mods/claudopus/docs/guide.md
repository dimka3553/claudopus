## Updates

Claudopus keeps itself up to date. Every hour one chat reads the version page on claudopus.ai (`https://www.claudopus.ai/version.json`), and when it names a version other than the one installed, asks Claude Code to fetch the newest release, as `/plugin` would. Every open chat then moves to the new version by itself, with Claude Code's own `/reload-plugins`, as soon as it has no turn of its own running; background work carries on through it. When a chat moves to a newer version, the octopus in the panel's header turns blue and jumps for five seconds, the header says "Claudopus updated" in blue and then the new version, and a short sound plays (once for each version on a Mac, however many chats move to it; the Config sound setting turns it off); then the header is as before. A chat looks for a newer installed copy every 20 seconds, reading Claude Code's own record of what is installed. To stay on the version you have, turn Automatic updates off in the Config tab. A copy loaded from a folder of its code (`--plugin-dir`) is never updated.

## What you see

- The mod is on in every chat you open from then on, in every folder.
- You send a message. The chat answers in one line and starts a background agent for it, then is free for your next message. A question that reading a few files answers, such as what a function does, the chat answers itself: it reads them in its own thread (Read, Grep, Glob) and starts no agent. Anything that changes a file, runs a command or takes more than a few reads still goes to an agent.
- Pieces of work running at once each spend their own tokens: five at a time spend about five times as fast as one. On a subscription that uses up your limits sooner; if you pay per token (an API key or other metered billing), it multiplies what you pay in that time.
- The panel opens beside the chat by itself the first time work starts in that chat. Each piece of work is a card with a line saying where it stands; its title opens its Details, and its coloured edge is its to-dos, one part each, brighter as they are done. When a piece of work starts helpers of its own, they hang inside its card as a tree joined by lines: each is one slim row with a dot that is purple while it works and green once it finished, its name, its model, its context and its time, and a click on a row opens that helper's own Details. Helpers that have ended fold into one row, and the work's Details lists every one. A piece of work's helpers run in the background, as the chat's own work does: the work carries on while they run, waits for them only when it has nothing else, with a command that comes back within about 20 seconds of one ending (or starting), or after two minutes, and is held back from finishing while one still runs (six tries at most, so it can never be stuck). Stopping the work stops its helpers too. While it has helpers running, its card says how many and when they were last looked in on, and that line turns amber when one has shown nothing new for five minutes, which the work is told. After each step the work is told when it could have sent its calls together: a helper started alone, three single lookups in a row, two long runs of one kind one after the other, a step of only to-dos, or a commit with no second look started yet. Once it is open in one chat it opens in every chat on this Mac, including chats that start later. Close it in one chat and it closes in all of them, and new work does not bring it back until `/claudopus` opens it again. Right after you install, it opens once by itself with a welcome that offers to index your old chats.
- The panel lists the running work of every open chat on this Mac, each chat's cards under that chat's name, this chat's first. You can answer another chat's question from here, and the answer reaches that chat's work. Stop, the model choice and the full record stay in the chat that runs the work. Work that ends in another chat turns green there for a moment and then folds into Finished, as in its own chat. Work that leaves the file of a chat still open without a word of how it ended (a chat on an older version) turns green for that moment too, then is gone. With `macs` set, every panel on each of your Macs lists the same chats, from all of them, each marked with the Mac it runs on. A chat is listed only while it has something going on (running work, a question or a prompt); chats with nothing going on are not listed at all, however many are open, since the app's own list of chats has them. Press a listed chat's name to open that chat in the Claude app, as its sidebar does: a chat this Mac's app holds (one of this Mac's, or one it runs on another Mac over SSH) opens by the app's own link, any other by its Remote Control link when Remote Control is on in it. A chat of another Mac's own app with Remote Control off opens only there. In a chat your Mac's app runs on another Mac over SSH, a press on a name is left for your Mac, which opens that chat on your screen within about two seconds. Beside the name, the header shows the version this chat loaded (dim), and for the few seconds before the chat reloads, the newer one your Mac has already installed (read from Claude Code's own record), so chats on two Macs on different versions are told apart at a glance. The header's line counts every chat the panel lists, so it says what the cards under it say: what needs you first (in amber, the octopus amber too, though happy for the moment any work finishes), then what runs here and in other chats, then "Just finished" for a moment, else "Nothing is running".
- When work needs your hands, its card turns amber and rises to the top with what to do, what it is about right under it (what a release changes, what a choice costs: whole in its Details, and on the small card up to about a screen), so an ask does not point at an explanation still on its way in the work's report; work that asks "as explained in the chat" without that explanation is refused and told to ask again with it, and a go-ahead asked with nothing under it is said back to the work. A question longer than 300 letters is cut at a word, with the whole of it under it. Above the prompt, an ask that has more says it is in the chat and on its card. Then any text to paste beside a Copy button (work asks you to type only a command it cannot run itself, given whole with its folder; on the Mac you sit at it also has Open in Terminal, which opens Terminal with it typed at the prompt in its folder, never run, and only when you press it; in a chat on another Mac there is no such button, and you type it in your own session to that Mac), and three ways to answer right there: the work's own buttons (or Done and Not now when it gave none), and a box to reply in your own words. Any answer clears the amber at once, the card says what you answered, and the work carries on from it at its next step; it turns amber again only if it asks again. An ask stays until you answer it, even after the work that made it has finished; then your answer starts that work again. The ask is also shown in the chat that started the work, as a notice from Claudopus with what it is about, its answers and the paths of any pictures it is about (the chat shows the pictures in its next reply), never as a message in your place; answer there in your own words and the chat passes it to the work, which counts as an answer on the card. An answer pressed on the card is noted in the chat too. It also raises a notification with the amber octopus, from a second small app of the mod's, Claudopus Asks, and a short "needs you" sound (two quick notes, then a higher one) that is part of the notification, so macOS plays it as you set Claudopus Asks in System Settings, Notifications, and stays silent under a Focus that holds it back. One ask is heard once, and asks that arrive within five seconds of each other on one Mac are heard as one; the first one makes macOS ask you once to allow Claudopus Asks, and is heard by the mod's own chime while macOS asks.
- When a chat stops on the app's own prompt (allow a command or a tool, answer its question, approve its plan), the panel in every chat shows it in amber at the top, under that chat's name, in plain words and never a tool's own name: which work asks (or the chat itself), what it asks, exactly what (the command, the message and the work it goes to, the page), what a yes and a no each do, and where to answer it. Once answered it is no longer amber: for a minute after the call ends the card says what you did and what is happening now (allowed and running, run and done, said no, or ended before you answered), then it goes. Claudopus's own calls that carry out what you already chose (your reply passed on to work, a Stop you pressed) never ask you again. A prompt still open when the app itself says it is waiting, a few seconds on, raises a notification, as a question from work does. Wherever a press on its name opens that chat, Open that chat takes you there to answer it; the panel never answers a prompt itself.
- A short band above the prompt says what is being done right now.
- A message about work that is already running goes to that work as a steer instead of starting new work.
- When work finishes, its card turns green with the happy octopus for a moment, then the chat passes on its result and the panel lists it under Finished. Work that gave its report while something it started in the background still ran counts as finished when it is stopped after; work stopped or failed before that does not turn green. Work that ends while the mod reloads gets its green moment once the panel draws again. What the work left for you to read is listed under Results.
- Stats, beside Results, adds up what the work came to, for this chat or every chat with kept work, today or all time: tokens by model, work finished, to-dos done, your messages and, on average, how long the chat took over one, from when it took it up to its last response or tool result before the next message (its own turn, without the background work it started), the time saved by running work side by side, a timeline, what the tokens would cost at API prices, and what the memory holds.
- Memory, at the top of the panel, opens Finder at the folder where Claudopus keeps what it remembers.
- About, beside Memory, waves hello once, says what Claudopus is and shows its version and Claude Code's, the website, the licence and the GitHub repo.
- A finish plays a short quiet chime, and a notification, with the purple octopus, names the work and the first words of its result, so you hear and see it when you are elsewhere. It comes from Claudopus with its octopus icon, and clicking it opens that chat in the Claude app (in a terminal, it brings the terminal forward). The first one makes macOS ask you once to allow Claudopus notifications.
- When work needs an admin command, a box at the top of the panel asks you once for that Mac's password, the same in a chat on this Mac and one run from another Mac over SSH (what you type shows as you type it); it stays in this Mac's keychain and is never put in a chat, a command line, a file or a log. Once it is kept, the password is not asked for again until you remove it with `bin/admin <mac> --forget`; each command still waits for your Allow, as the next line says. The chat that asked shows a notice naming only what is needed and brings the panel up; Cancel in the box ends the wait, and the work waits up to about 10 minutes for you. Only a run with no chat open on this Mac to draw a panel (a `claude -p` run) asks in a macOS dialog on this Mac's screen instead, and over SSH says that no panel is open.
- Before an admin command runs, its work's card asks you: it shows the exact command and the Mac, with Allow once, Always allow this command and Deny. Only your press allows it, in the panel of the chat the work runs in; nothing typed in a chat does, and other chats' panels show it without buttons. Deny tells the work you refused it. Always allow keeps that exact command for that Mac in `~/.claude/claudopus/admin-allowed.json`, and Config lists them, each with Remove. Config's "Ask before an admin command" (`admin`) chooses when you are asked: each time, as shipped; once for a piece of work and its helpers, until it ends; or never, which is how it was before this setting. The hook holds the command, and the admin tool itself runs nothing you did not allow, so a script, another spelling of its path or a command the shell builds is refused too.
- What you allowed, the always-allowed list and a "work" or "never" setting each count only with a seal your press made, under a key in this Mac's keychain; an entry or a setting written into those files by anything else allows nothing, removing one makes a new key so an old seal put back counts for nothing, and work is refused commands that name the list, the grants or that key, which stops slips, not every way there is. A kept password can be read by any process running as you, so only "Let macOS ask, keep nothing" is a wall: a process that reads the keychain can also make a seal. That choice (Config's Admin password, `adminPassword`) keeps no password and forgets any kept one: macOS asks for it in its own box on this Mac's screen each time a command runs, for commands on this Mac only, never on another Mac over SSH. That box is macOS's own and does not show the command; the card before it does. In a chat you reach from another device that box cannot be seen, so while it is set admin commands are refused there, with a line saying so.
- When work needs a key, token or password, a box at the top of the panel asks you once for it by name, in every chat; it stays in this Mac's keychain, work uses it as an environment variable, and the value is hidden where a command prints it by accident. A command that changes the value before printing it is not stopped, and the hook that refuses keychain reads catches the usual commands, not every way there is.
- The words in a password or key box are the panel's own, made from what the request names: a Mac's admin password or a key, the Mac or the key's name, and why it asks again. Any other text in a request is shown nowhere, and a request the panel cannot word is not drawn, so a request planted by work shows only what the runners themselves would ask. Work is refused commands that write into `~/.claude/claudopus/boxes` or read the boxes' keychain entries (`claudopus-box`), typed, redirected, in a script it runs or in another language's code, and files written there; only `bin/admin` and `bin/secret` use them. The field shows what you type because the app's text field, in the desktop app and the terminal, has no hidden mode. This stops slips and common tricks; it is no wall against code set on getting round it.
- Work changes a repo only in its own git worktree, on a branch of its own, and never pushes or merges to main itself: Claude Code refuses it (`git push` naming main or master, `gh pr merge`), and the repo's `pre-push` hook refuses a plain push of main from inside Claude Code. The one way there is `bin/release`, run from the work's worktree. When the work is built and checked it asks you whether to release it, with a Release it button; on your answer, on the card or typed in the chat, the work runs the release itself, and you never type it. On one Mac one release per repo runs at a time and the rest wait their turn (`release --status` says who is releasing); across Macs, a release whose push git refuses takes main again. It merges the work's branch onto main as it is now, numbers the version from main, makes the build's files again rather than merging them, refuses files the build left that the repo does not track, runs the repo's checks, then pushes main and the version's tag. When main and the branch changed the same lines it pushes nothing and names what landed on main, for the work to merge and run it again. A repo says how in a `.release` file: `NAME`, `VERSION_FILE` (a JSON file with `"version"`, or one holding only the number: main's number plus one, the rest of the file merged as any file), `DERIVED` (files the build makes), `BUILD`, `CHECK`, `AFTER` (run once pushed), `GITHUB_RELEASE` (a command that prints a version's release notes: with it every version is also made a GitHub release marked latest, `gh` must be signed in before anything is pushed, and a release whose tag or GitHub release fails after the push exits 4 with the one command that finishes it) and `MAIN` (else the remote's own default branch); main's own `.release` is the one used, so a branch cannot drop its checks, and `release.local` in the repo's git folder sets the same for one Mac. A repo without one releases with no version or checks, and a repo with no main yet gets the branch as its first main. When the remote refuses a push for another reason, such as a main that takes only pull requests, it says why and pushes nothing.
- `/servers` shows the local servers running on your machine and which chat started each.
- A chat your Mac's Claude app runs on another Mac over SSH keeps working when your Mac goes away. The app owns that chat's process there and its sign-in: it may end the process (it did when a closed laptop came back for a moment overnight), and with the app away the sign-in it lends lapses. So in such a chat Claudopus starts a small keeper on that Mac, which outlives the process. When the process ends while work is at work and the app does not start the chat again within a minute, the keeper starts the same chat again there, as one of Claude Code's own background sessions, under that Mac's own sign-in, with the same model, effort, permission mode and plugins and with Remote Control on. When the process still runs but the sign-in the app lent it has lapsed (two turns within ten minutes end with the API's own 401 for it), Claudopus has the keeper end that process, and the chat goes on there at once, without the minute's wait. Claude Code starts the cut-off work again itself; whatever the keeper's copy could not start, Claudopus sends a message that does. Each such card says "Cut off at 00:14 as the chat's process ended; going again since 00:15", or, in amber, that it could not be started again; one notice says the same in the chat ("… has been going on this Mac by itself since 00:15"), on that Mac and, where Claude Code's push is on (`agentPushNotifEnabled`) and Remote Control is connected, on your phone. While the copy runs you can watch it from the Claude app's Remote Control or from a terminal: `ssh <that Mac> -t claude attach <its id>` (`claude agents` lists it, named after the chat with "(kept on this Mac)"). Once its work is done and it has been idle two minutes, the keeper stops the copy, and the chat opens in the app as before, with all that ran meanwhile in it and on its cards. If you open the chat in the app while the copy still works, the keeper stops the copy at once so the app can have the chat; the app's first try may say the chat is running in the background: send your message again, and Claudopus starts the cut-off work again in the app's chat. Work started with a command held open loses that command when a process ends, so work starts anything longer than a few minutes detached and follows its log; that goes on through every change. The keeper's record of each chat is in `keep/`. The app ends the process the same way when you archive the chat or quit the app, so work still at work then goes on too: stop it first if you do not want it to. A chat this Mac's own terminal runs, as in tmux, is never ended by the app and needs no keeper.
- In the terminal the panel opens one other tab beside it at a time (Results, a result, Stats, Memory, Config, About, the Changelog, a card's Details). The tab's first row is `x: Close`: click it, or press `x` while the tab holds the keys (`ctrl+x Tab` gives them to the panel's frame, `Esc` gives them back to the chat), and the tab closes and the panel comes back. Claude Code's own row of tab names above it switches between the panel and the tab by a click, and its `✕` at the frame's top right closes the tab shown. Clicks work in a terminal reached over SSH and inside tmux, as long as Claude Code runs full screen (`"tui": "fullscreen"`). In a narrow terminal, under 144 columns as on a phone, where the panel sits above the prompt, a tab a button opens is drawn there too, and closing it leaves the panel in place.

The mod also adds a short set of working principles ([rules/CLAUDE.md](../rules/CLAUDE.md)) to every chat. They shape how Claude answers. They leave out what is one person's own call, such as when Claude may raise a concern or what counts as done; add yours, or change any of them, in the Config tab, and your copy is the one your chats carry.

The chat picks a model for each piece of work: Opus for most, Sonnet for long legwork, Fable only for heavy work. A check of finished work runs on Opus, or on Fable for work built on Fable, never on Sonnet. Your account needs access to the model it picks.

No work runs on an older model or on Haiku (unless you name Haiku in `models`). An agent started on one, by the chat or by a piece of work, is refused. When the app itself moves running work to an older model, as it does after its safeguards flag a message (its setting "Switch models when a message is flagged"), each step of that work goes to Fable 5.2 instead (an older Sonnet, or Haiku, goes to Sonnet 5.5), and while that lasts the work's own card says which model it was kept off; a helper's says so once opened, and `log.md` has a line for each. If the model it goes to flags the message too, the work stops. A card's model is the one that really answered its last step; if that is still an older model, the card says it is not a model work should run on. The chat's own thread stays on the model you picked for it, and with `agents` off none of this applies.

## Needs

- Claude Code 2.1.287 or newer in the terminal, or the desktop app 2.1.286 or newer. The panel draws in VS Code from 2.1.299.
- Does not work on Windows under WSL (plugins do not load there) or where your organization's admins block mods. In `claude -p` print mode and cloud sessions there is no panel.
- On a Mac (12 or newer): the password and key boxes, the keychain, thumbnails and notifications use `osascript`, `security`, `sips`, `open` and two small apps of the mod's, `notifier/Claudopus.app` and `notifier/ClaudopusAsks.app`, which are copied to `~/Library/Application Support/Claudopus` on its first notification. If you turn Claudopus Asks off in System Settings, Notifications, what needs you comes as Claudopus, purple, with the mod's own chime instead of the notification's sound; if you turn Claudopus off, macOS's plain notification is used instead, which cannot show the icon or open the chat.
- Stats reads Claude's records with `jq`, which macOS 15 and later include.
- The two notification apps come built, so you need no Xcode; how to rebuild them and compare with what is committed is in [What Claudopus does on your Mac](privacy.md#files-that-will-be-held-for-a-reviewer-and-why-they-stay).
- Seen working in the macOS desktop app and in a terminal on Linux (Ubuntu 24.04). VS Code has not been tried yet.

## On Linux

Claude Code runs on Linux in a terminal (there is no Claude desktop app for Linux), and Claudopus installs there with the same lines as on a Mac. The panel, background work, results, memory, updates, admin commands and keys all work; where a Mac has something of its own, Linux does this instead:

- **Passwords and keys.** Linux has no keychain a terminal program can use without a desktop signed in, so `bin/keyring` keeps each one in a file of its own under `~/.local/share/claudopus/keyring` (or `$XDG_DATA_HOME/claudopus/keyring`), the folders readable only by you (700) and the files too (600). As on a Mac, any program running as you can read them; work is refused commands that name that folder.
- **"Let macOS ask, keep nothing"** is a Mac's own box, so on Linux it is not there: set to it, an admin command does not run and says to keep the password instead. Admin commands use `sudo` as on a Mac.
- **A password or key with no panel open** (a `claude -p` run): a Mac asks in a box on its screen; Linux has none, so the runner says to open the panel with `/claudopus` and run it again.
- **Notifications** use the desktop's own (`notify-send`) when Claude Code runs in a desktop session. In a terminal with no desktop, as over SSH, there is no pop-up: the panel, its sound and the terminal's own notice are what you get.
- **Opening things.** Memory, a picture or a settings file opens with `xdg-open` on a desktop; with none, Memory opens as a tab of the panel and a picture's row says where it is, or sends it to your device as it does over SSH from a Mac. A card's Open in Terminal button is not there: copy the command instead.
- **Small copies of pictures** are made with ImageMagick (`sudo apt install imagemagick`, or your system's own) by `bin/sips`, which answers the calls the mod makes of macOS's `sips`. Without it, pictures are listed without their small copies.
- **Your other Macs** (`macs`) and the keeper of a chat the app runs over SSH work the same; this machine is named by `hostname`.
- **Not on Linux:** the two notification apps (`notifier/`), which are Mac apps, and what reads the desktop app's own folders and logs, which Linux does not have; nothing is shown for them.

## Settings

The panel's Config tab lists everything you can set, shows what is in force, and puts each back to what shipped. Edit on the principles, the chat's rules or the work rules makes your own copy at the same path under `~/.claude/claudopus` (`rules/CLAUDE.md`, `roles/chat.md`, `roles/work.md`); the mod uses your copy when there is one, and an update never touches it.

The settings are `~/.claude/claudopus/config.json`. Every key is optional:

```json
{
  "name": "Sam",
  "agents": true,
  "sound": true,
  "notifications": true,
  "updates": true,
  "admin": "each",
  "adminPassword": "keep",
  "models": { "work": "opus", "legwork": "sonnet", "heavy": "fable" },
  "macs": ["sam@other-mac.local"]
}
```

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | What the agents call you. Left out, they say "the person". |
| `agents` | `false` stops the handing over; every chat then does its own work again. |
| `sound` | `false` silences the short chime played when work is ready, and the "needs you" sound of a notification that work needs you. |
| `notifications` | `false` stops the notification that names work when it is ready, stopped or failed. |
| `updates` | `false` stops Claudopus updating itself; it stays on the version you have until you update it with `/plugin`. |
| `admin` | When you are asked before an admin command runs: `each` time, once for a piece of `work` and its helpers until it ends, or `never`. `work` and `never` count only when chosen in Config, which seals them (`adminSealed`); written by hand, every command still asks. |
| `adminPassword` | `macos` lets macOS ask for the admin password each time and keeps none, on this Mac only; left out or `keep`, it is asked once in the panel and kept in the keychain. |
| `models` | Which model runs most work, long legwork and helpers, and heavy work (`opus`, `sonnet`, `fable` or `haiku`). Checks of finished work run on the most-work or heavy-work model, never on legwork's. |
| `macs` | Up to four other Macs, as `user@host` or a `Host` of `~/.ssh/config`, that this Mac's panel lists the chats of. It uses your own SSH, which must log in without asking; each Mac needs Claudopus of this version or newer. That Mac's panels list this Mac's chats in turn, with nothing to set there: each visit hands it this Mac's chats and brings back the answers pressed there for them. Results are kept the same on both, about every two minutes: each Mac gets the results it lacks, with paths moved onto its own home, and what you did to a result (archived, brought back, answered) reaches the other; nothing is deleted or overwritten. |

`name`, `agents` and `macs` are read when a chat starts; `sound` and `notifications` each time work ends or needs you; `updates` before each look for a new version.

## Turn it off

```
/plugin disable claudopus@claudopus
```

`/plugin enable claudopus@claudopus` turns it back on; `/plugin uninstall claudopus@claudopus` removes it. Your memory folder is left in place.

## Memory

Everything the mod keeps is in `~/.claude/claudopus`, as plain files:

| Path | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `index.md` | The subjects of your work, one line each |
| `streams/` | One brief per subject |
| `words/` | Your messages, word for word, and Claude's answers, one file per chat |
| `asks/` | What you asked in each chat and what was done, one file per chat |
| `reports/` | What finished work left for you, one dated folder each, with copies of the pictures it named and small thumbnails |
| `finished/` | The Finished list of each chat |
| `flows/` | Each workflow run the panel follows |
| `pastes/` | A message over 12,000 characters, kept here while the chat gets its start, its end and this path |
| `stats/`, `thumbs/` | Sums Stats made of Claude Code's records, and small copies of pictures shown in the panel |
| `servers.json` | The servers `/servers` last saw |
| `config.json` | Your settings, written by the Config tab |
| `admin-allowed.json` | The admin commands you chose Always allow for, by Mac, each sealed; Config lists them |
| `admin-grants/` | What you just allowed, for the admin tool to take once: gone as the command starts or its call ends; one a call sent to the background never took stops counting after two minutes and is cleared later |
| `welcome.json` | Whether the first-run welcome still shows |
| `panel.json` | Whether you last opened or closed the panel, which every chat follows |
| `live/` | Each open chat's running work and its Remote Control and app ids, rewritten at least every 20 seconds, for the other chats' panels and the panels of other Macs; answers left for a chat from another panel; and, with `macs`, what was last fetched from each of those Macs; on a Mac named by another, the chats that Mac handed over (`live/pushed/`) and the answers waiting for it (`live/outbox/`). A chat removes its own file when it closes |
| `rules/CLAUDE.md`, `roles/chat.md`, `roles/work.md` | Your own copies of the three texts, written only when you edit or save one in the Config tab; Reset moves your copy aside as `.old` |
| `update.json`, `reloads/` | What the last look for a new version found, and each chat's last reload to a newer installed version, so none reloads twice for the same one within ten minutes |
| `keep/` | For a chat the app runs from another Mac: what is at work (`<chat>.json`), and the keeper's times and log (`<chat>.state`, `<chat>.log`) |
| `log.md` | The mod's own log |

Delete the folder to start over. The mod writes nowhere else: no build, start-up or settings file of Claude Code or your projects.
